Why study system dynamics?

Single pendulum. Two equilibria.

These are two resting positions of the same rigid-rod pendulum. The mass, length, gravity and pivot damping are identical, and no external torque is applied. Both satisfy the same equation of motion. Why does a small nudge have such different consequences?

Solid: equilibrium. Dashed: a small displacement. Yellow: gravity. Both resting positions satisfy the equation; their stability differs.

01 · Equation

Equation of motion

For a point mass m on a massless rigid rod of length ℓ, I=mℓ2. Measure θ from downward. Take viscous damping b>0 and no applied torque.

Iθ̈ = −bθ̇ − mgℓ sinθ
Iθ̈ + bθ̇ + mgℓ sinθ = 0

Local equations

Let δ=θ−θ* be a small angular deviation. Downward: θ*=0, sinθ≈δ. Upright: θ*=π, sinθ≈−δ.

Downward

Iδ̈ + bδ̇ + mgℓδ = 0

Upright

Iδ̈ + bδ̇ − mgℓδ = 0
02 · Solve the poles

Poles and stability

Take the Laplace transform: Δ(s)=ℒ{δ(t)}, δ0=δ(0), v0=δ̇(0). Set the denominator to zero, with I=mℓ2.

Downward

Δ(s) = (Is+b)δ0 + Iv0Is2 + bs + mgℓ
s2 + (b/I)s + g/ℓ = 0
s1,2 = −b/(2I) ± √[(b/(2I))2 − g/ℓ]

Both poles have negative real parts.
Both modes decay: locally asymptotically stable.

Upright

Δ(s) = (Is+b)δ0 + Iv0Is2 + bs − mgℓ
s2 + (b/I)s − g/ℓ = 0
s1,2 = −b/(2I) ± √[(b/(2I))2 + g/ℓ]

One positive pole, one negative pole.
The growing mode makes the equilibrium unstable.

Numerical poles

m=1 kg, ℓ=1 m, g=9.81 m/s2, b=0.8 N·m·s/rad; I=1 kg·m2.
