Lecture 1 · slide 24: equations and symbols

Small-motion model: valve mass flow q1 is proportional to valve displacement x. Oil density ρ and effective piston area A are constant.

q1 = K1x
ρA dy/dt = K1x

x, y [m]: valve-spool and piston displacement.
ps, p0 [Pa]: supply and return pressure.
q1, q2 [kg/s]: mass flow at the two cylinder ports; positive q1 extends the cylinder.
ρ [kg/m³], A [m²]: oil density and effective piston area.
K1 [kg/(s·m)]: local valve gain dq1/dx.

The valve control shows x/xmax as a percentage; it does not specify an absolute spool stroke. The flow readout shows |q1| = ρA|dy/dt|. Fixed x gives nearly constant piston speed in the slide model.

Animation assumptions and pressure calculation

The animation uses equal effective cylinder areas, incompressible oil and a viscous load. It illustrates nonlinear metering; q1 = K1x is the slide’s local small-motion approximation. Fluid-pressure transients, inertia and leakage are omitted. Hold is an ideal closed-centre condition; an end stop supplies a reaction force.

q1 = ρA dy/dt
F = A(pA − pB) = b dy/dt (away from an end stop)

A = 20 cm²; b = 20,000 N·s/m; ρ = 850 kg/m³; discharge coefficient Cd = 0.65; maximum opening area per orifice = 4 mm²; p0 = 1 bar. Positive x opens the valve to extend the cylinder.

For each open metering restriction, mass-flow magnitude is |q| = Cda√(2ρΔp). The two restriction pressure drops plus the cylinder pressure difference equal ps − p0. Neutral pressure transients are not resolved.
